Until now, every answer was a list of rows. Today you ask for a summary instead: how many, what is the total, what is the average. You already know these ideas from a café receipt.

You say to the waiter “Please could you tell me how many drinks are on the menu, what they cost altogether and the average price? Thank you!”
↓ same thing in SQL
SELECT COUNT(*), SUM(price), AVG(price) FROM menu WHERE type = 'drink';
The one thing to remember

The receipt words

A café receipt already ends with how many items you bought and the total. Our receipt adds three more lines at the bottom. Those five lines are today's five new SQL words. Here is the receipt for one of each drink on our menu.

COUNTHow many? Counts the rows.
SUMWhat is the total? Adds up a number column.
AVGWhat is the average? The total shared out equally: £14.50 ÷ 4 = £3.625, which rounds to £3.63.
MINWhat is the smallest? The cheapest, the lowest.
MAXWhat is the biggest? The most expensive, the highest.
What they all do

Many rows in, one row out

All five receipt words work the same way. They take all the rows that are left and squash them into one row. Remember this little picture:

8 rows go in
→
1 row comes out
WHERE still works first. The database throws away rows with WHERE, and only then squashes what is left. So WHERE type = 'drink' followed by COUNT counts only the drinks.
One thing you can't do yet: SELECT item, COUNT(*). COUNT squashes 8 rows into 1, so which item name should that one row show? There is no right answer, so most databases give you an error. (A few let it through and show any item at all, which is even more confusing.) Lesson 4 (GROUP BY) shows how to do it properly.
The new symbols

Brackets mean “of”

Each receipt word needs to know what to work on. You put that inside round brackets, straight after the word. When you read it out loud, say the brackets as “of”.

COUNT(*)“the count of every row”The star inside means “rows, whatever is in them”. Gives 8.
SUM(price)“the sum of price”Adds every price together. Gives £30.50.
AVG(calories)“the average of calories”Total calories ÷ number of rows: 1645 ÷ 8. Gives 205.625.
Words or numbers? SUM and AVG need a number column (price or calories), because you can't add up words. COUNT, MIN and MAX work on anything: MIN(item) gives the name that comes first from A to Z.
